On-page SEO is everything you control on the page itself. Nothing here is a trick. It is simply making the page clear for people first and for search engines second. Run through this list before you hit publish.

## Content and intent

- Decide what one question or task the page serves.
- Answer it early, then go deeper.
- Use the words your customers use, including your main keyword, once or twice naturally.
- Add something original: your experience, examples, photos or numbers.
- Show who wrote it and when it was updated.

## Titles and descriptions

- Give every page its own title, ideally under about 60 characters, with the main topic near the start.
- Write a meta description of roughly 120 to 155 characters that earns the click. It may not always be shown, but it helps.

## Structure

- One h1 per page. Use h2 and h3 to organise the rest.
- Short, readable URLs, for example /services/seo.
- Link to related pages with descriptive anchor text, not click here.

## Images and media

- Compress images and set width and height to avoid layout jumps.
- Write alt text that describes what the image shows.
- Lazy-load images below the fold.

## Technical basics

- The page loads fast on mobile and is mobile friendly.
- It is reachable by links and listed in your sitemap.
- The canonical tag points to the right address.
- Add structured data where it fits, such as organisation, article or FAQ.

## After publishing

Check the page in Google Search Console, request indexing and review performance after a few weeks, then improve what is not working.

## What this means for your business

We build these basics into every site we make, so SEO is not an afterthought. If you already have a site, a short audit against this list usually finds quick wins.
