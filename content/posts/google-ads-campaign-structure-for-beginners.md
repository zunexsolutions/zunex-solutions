Most wasted ad spend comes from a messy setup, not from Google Ads itself. A tidy structure makes everything easier: writing relevant ads, controlling cost and understanding what works. Menus and names change over time, so check Google's help pages for the latest screens.

## The structure

An account holds campaigns. Each campaign has a budget, location and goal. Inside it, ad groups hold a small set of closely related keywords and the ads that match them.

## Pick the right campaign type

- Search: text ads when people look for what you sell. The best starting point for most local and service businesses.
- Performance Max: automated across Google's channels. Needs good conversion data and assets.
- Shopping: product listings for online stores.
- Display and YouTube: awareness and retargeting.

## Keywords and match types

Broad match reaches the widest set of searches, phrase match requires the meaning to be included and exact match is the tightest. Start tighter, then widen once you see real search terms.

## Negative keywords

Add terms you do not want to show for, such as free, jobs or DIY. Reviewing the search terms report weekly and adding negatives is the single easiest way to cut waste.

## Write ads people want to click

Responsive search ads let you supply up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions. Include the keyword, a clear benefit and one call to action. Make sure the landing page delivers exactly what the ad promised, because that affects your Quality Score, which Google rates from 1 to 10.

## Set up conversion tracking first

Before spending, track what counts: calls, form submissions, purchases. Without it, bidding strategies have nothing to learn from and you cannot tell what pays.

## Budget and patience

Start with a daily budget you can afford to lose while learning. Give automated bidding time to gather data before judging it, and change one thing at a time.
