A logo is not a brand. A brand is what people expect and remember when they deal with you. Brand identity is the set of deliberate choices, visual and verbal, that make that impression consistent.

## Start with the thinking

Before touching colours, answer these in plain sentences.

- Who exactly are you for?
- What problem do you solve, and why are you better or different?
- What three words should customers use to describe you?
- What do you refuse to be?

The answers become your positioning. Everything visual should support them.

## The visual identity

- Logo: a primary version, a compact version and a one-colour version.
- Colour: one main colour, one or two supports and neutral tones. Check contrast so text stays readable.
- Typography: one font for headings and one for body text is plenty.
- Imagery: decide the style of photos and graphics, and stick to it.
- Layout: consistent spacing and alignment make you look professional.

## Build a simple brand kit

Put the essentials in one short document: logo files, colour codes, fonts, voice notes and a few examples. Anyone who designs for you, from a freelancer to a printer, should be able to follow it.

## Common mistakes

- Copying a competitor, so you blend in.
- Using too many colours and fonts.
- Changing the look every few months.
- Forgetting the experience: replies, packaging, invoices and how you answer the phone are part of the brand.

## Consistency beats perfection

A good, simple identity used everywhere beats a brilliant one used in half the places. Check your website, social profiles, documents and signage for matches.
