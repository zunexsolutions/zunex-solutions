Brand voice is how your business sounds in writing. Tone changes with the situation, but voice stays recognisable. Without one, your website sounds formal, your Instagram sounds silly and your emails sound like someone else entirely.

## Voice versus tone

Voice is your personality: warm, direct, curious. Tone is how you dial it for the moment. You would write differently to apologise for a delay than to announce a launch, but both should sound like you.

## Choose three or four traits

For each trait, say what it means and what it does not. For example:

- Clear: we use short sentences and everyday words. We do not hide behind jargon.
- Warm: we are friendly and respectful. We do not use slang or sarcasm.
- Confident: we say what we know. We do not exaggerate or make promises we cannot keep.

## Show it with examples

A before-and-after pair teaches faster than any description. Take a real sentence from your website and rewrite it in your voice. Keep five to ten of these.

## Add a few practical rules

- Words we like and words we avoid.
- How we address the customer.
- How we handle numbers, dates and names.
- Whether we use emoji, and where.

## Adapt by channel

Your website can be calm and complete. A reel caption should be short and lively. A support reply should be patient and specific. Write a line for each channel on how the voice flexes.

## Test it

Give an unlabelled paragraph to a colleague and ask whether it sounds like your company. Ask a customer the same. Adjust until it does.

## What this means for your business

A voice guide of one or two pages is enough. We help clients draft one, then apply it across the website, ads and social content.
