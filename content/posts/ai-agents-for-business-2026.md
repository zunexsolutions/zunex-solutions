Two years ago an AI agent was a conference demo. This year it is a budget line. Gartner expects roughly 40% of enterprise applications to include task-specific agents by the end of 2026, up from under 5% in 2025, and KPMG's Q1 2026 pulse survey found 54% of organisations actively deploying agents in core operations.

## Take the big numbers with some salt

Adoption figures come from surveys, and surveys flatter. CrewAI's poll of 500 executives says every respondent plans to expand agent use, which tells you more about who answered than about the market. Still, the direction is hard to argue with. The first wave is IT, customer support and finance, where work is repetitive, rule-heavy and easy to check.

## Why projects stall

Mostly integration. One 2026 report has 46% of organisations naming connections to existing systems as their main obstacle. The model is rarely the problem; the CRM with no API is. Permissions come next: an agent that can read invoices should not automatically approve them. Last comes trust. People need to see what the agent did and undo it quickly.

## A sensible way to start

Skip the grand rollout.

- Pick one workflow with a cost you can measure, such as lead follow-up or ticket triage.
- Keep a human approval step for anything that spends money or contacts a customer.
- Log every action the agent takes.
- Review time saved after a month, then decide whether to expand.

## What this means for your business

At Zunex we build these for small and mid-sized teams, usually an assistant or automation wired into tools you already use: a CRM, WhatsApp, a spreadsheet. If you can describe the task in one sentence and measure it every week, it is probably a good first candidate.
