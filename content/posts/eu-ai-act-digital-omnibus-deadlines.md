For a while, August 2, 2026 was the date everyone circled for the EU AI Act. Then the Digital Omnibus on AI arrived. The Council gave final approval on June 29, 2026, and the amending regulation was published on July 24 and took effect on July 27, days before the original deadline.

## The new dates

Stand-alone high-risk systems (the Annex III list, which covers areas like hiring and credit scoring) now have until December 2, 2027. AI built into regulated products such as medical devices and machinery gets until August 2, 2028. Law firms covering the change are clear that the requirements themselves did not shrink; only the calendar did.

## What did not move

Rules already in force stay in force: the bans on prohibited practices and the AI literacy duties from February 2025, and the obligations for general-purpose AI model providers from August 2025. Article 50 transparency duties, such as telling people when they are talking to an AI system, are described as largely unaffected. Fines for high-risk breaches can still reach EUR 15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover once they apply.

## Do you need to care?

If you sell to customers in the EU or build features that affect them, yes, at least to the extent of knowing which bucket you are in.

- List every AI feature you ship or use.
- Check whether any falls under a high-risk category.
- Make sure chatbots and generated content are clearly labelled.
- Keep notes on how your system works. You will want them later.

## What this means for your business

We are not lawyers and this is not legal advice. What we can do is build AI features with logging, human oversight and clear labelling from the start, which makes any later compliance work much cheaper.
